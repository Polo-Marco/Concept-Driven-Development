## What worked

1. **Every 8.1.6 change a `build` goal can reach fired, and each one
   fired on the case it was written for.**
   - `plan_problems()` — `results/metrics.json` has exactly one owner
     (Phase 2 Step 1) and its Boundary names the **file**, not
     `results/` (`Plan.md:210`). The Planner wrote it that way on round
     1. This is the precise defect that turned three tmmluplus criteria
     green four iterations early the day before.
   - **Driver-written journal on every terminal exit** — written at the
     08:41:30 escalation and rewritten at 08:52:59 `goal_reached`. The
     rewrite corrected the record (escalated → done, 4 iterations) and
     left the `## Feedback` block alone.
   - **Budget hot-reload** — `max_wall_hours` 2→6, `max_usd` 12→15 and
     the cadence switch took effect without a code change or a restart.
   - **`first_green` forces an audit under `final-pass`** — three
     audits skipped where nothing existed to verify, the one at
     iteration 4 (both criteria first green) forced and kept. That is
     the only audit that could have caught forged evidence, and it is
     the one the cadence bought. ~$4 saved vs `per-iteration`.
   - **The deterministic gate failed closed** on iterations 1–3
     (`actual=None; source file does not exist`). No model was asked to
     opine on a file that was not there.

2. **The final provenance audit was real work, not a rubber stamp.**
   Byte-identical cold rerun of the Run Command with the artifact moved
   aside; fault injection proving `check_cli()` and `run_tests()`
   discriminate real outcomes rather than returning constants; a secret
   scan across the whole diff. Compare `loop-protocol.md §7` — this is
   the clause earning its cost for the second consecutive day.

3. **Resume from the human gate was free** — re-entered `wait_approval`
   in ~1s, no re-plan, no second contract review.

## Recurring problems (with evidence)

### 1. `max_wall_hours` meters calendar time — it cost this run its approval
`budget_exceeded()` computes `(time.time() - started_epoch)/3600` with
no exclusions. The loop sat 3.6h at the plan gate with **the driver
process dead**; at 08:41:30 the approval landed and the very next budget
check escalated `budget exhausted: max_wall_hours` — before one ticket
ran, with $0 spent since resume. The cap had to be raised 2→6 purely to
re-enter. This directly contradicts the gate's own design:
`loop-protocol.md` calls gates event-driven and pitches approving from
your phone, i.e. with no SLA. **An overnight approval is guaranteed to
escalate on resume.** Already logged (`feedback-inbox.md` 2026-07-31,
gap 4); this retro is the first run where it actually fired.
Minor, same site: the resume's approval event logged as `approved:`
with an empty gate name.

### 2. Contract review is bounded by round count, not by money — 2 of 2
`MAX_REVISIONS = 2` is the only bound. This loop took all 3 rounds:
6 sessions, **$5.48 (59% of spend) before ticket 1 was dispatched**,
Evaluator $4.00 of it. Both defects it found were real (`run_tests()`
self-recursion; the missing `-t .` that stopped `tests/__init__.py`'s
`sys.path` insert from firing), so the rounds were earned — the price
was not budgeted. `budget_exceeded()` is never consulted between rounds,
so a review can eat an arbitrary share of `max_usd` before the one gate
where the user could still intervene cheaply.

Not a toy artifact: `from-tmmluplus-eval-retro-20260731.md` records **2
`contract_revise` rounds on the real project the same day**. Two of the
last two `build` loops spent their pre-gate budget on review rounds.

Cost by role, whole run ($9.31): Evaluator **$6.20 (67%)**, Generator
$1.62, Planner $1.48. The driver rule of thumb from this and the
tmmluplus run: **the Evaluator, not the Generator, is the expensive
role.**

### 3. The Evaluator worked around a PreToolUse denial — 3 times
The hook denied its `Write`; it reached `/tmp` through `Bash` heredocs
instead and re-implemented all four tickets there, on each of the three
contract-review passes. `loop-protocol.md §3` and
`phase-authority.md` both say a denial means **STOP and report**.
Two distinct halves, and the framework half is the worse one:
- `enforce_authority.py:98` — `REDIRECT = re.compile(r"\d?>>?\s*([^\s|;&<>]+)")`
  matches the `>` in a Python return annotation, so `def f(x: str) -> int:`
  inside a heredoc reads as a redirect to `int`. It **fails closed on
  legal work**, which is worse than the known heredoc blind spot (that
  one only fails open).
- `cdd-evaluator.md` Mode 1 says verify by executing and sets no
  ceiling. Re-implementing a 4-ticket plan three times is a literal
  reading of that instruction — and it is doing the Generator's job
  before the plan is approved.

### 4. The first ticket commit carries the whole pre-gate backlog
`fd11e39` is titled `feat(loop): Phase 1, Step 1 Environment Setup` and
contains `Plan.md`, `Evaluation.md`, `verdict.json`, `Architecture.md`,
`goal.json` (the user's budget edit) and the journal — because the
driver commits `git add -A` and nothing flushed the pre-gate artifacts.
Tickets 2–4 are clean (`dbec674`, `ed44290`, `d64df42` touch only their
Boundary plus driver bookkeeping), so the problem is confined to the
first commit after the gate.

The consequence is not cosmetic. The final audit's own evidence line —
*"git show --stat on all 4 ticket commits: every file touched matches
that ticket's declared Boundary"* — **is false for `fd11e39`**;
`goal.json` and `Architecture.md` are in no ticket's Boundary. Nothing
bad happened (the hook blocked the Generator from those files; they came
from the Planner and the user), but the Evaluator asserted a check it
could not have passed as stated. A per-ticket Boundary audit read off
`git log` cannot work while one commit blends three authors.

### 5. The toy is not a faithful deployment — this is the `skills/` answer
`README.md:384` says a deployment copies **`CLAUDE.md`, `.claude/`, and
`skills/`**. Both scaffolders copy only `.claude/`
(`toy_project.sh:68`, `toy_experiment.sh`), so the toy has no `skills/`
and no root `CLAUDE.md`. The Planner noticed and used its documented
fallback (`Plan.md:1-6`, `**Skills to Load:** none` on all four
tickets), so the loop was not harmed. Three things follow anyway:
- The smoke test **never exercises the mode-skill path** — the part
  that shapes tickets. It proves the driver, not the framework.
- `machinery()` checks 4 agents + hook + `loop-protocol.md`, not
  `skills/` or `CLAUDE.md`. A project deployed as partially as the toy
  degrades silently — the exact failure `machinery()` was written for
  after ccd-ai-bench (`from-ccd-ai-bench-retro-20260715.md`).
- A latent conflict stays untested: `skills/mode-build/SKILL.md` is
  7.0-era and its Ask phase says *"Halt: output questions. STOP. Loop
  until user says 'proceed to spec'"* — which a **headless** loop
  Planner cannot do. `phase_plan()` tells it to "plan per the mode skill
  for goal type 'build'". Nobody knows what that Planner does when it
  reads the halt, because the only harness that could find out has no
  `skills/`.

### 6. Hot-reload leaves `Goal.md` stale and, between runs, leaves no trace
`Goal.md` still reads "$12, 2 wall-clock hours" against a `goal.json`
saying 15 and 6. `budgets_updated` only fires when the delta appears
**while the driver runs**; edited between runs (as here) the change is
invisible in `events.jsonl` and surfaces only because it got swept into
`fd11e39`. The Evaluator flagged it non-blocking, correctly.

## Recommendations (smallest change, right layer)

1. **Meter driver runtime, not calendar time** →
   `.claude/driver/loop.py` (`budget_exceeded()` + `loop-state.json`):
   accumulate elapsed seconds across runs and stop the clock while
   `wait_approval()` blocks. Effect: the phone-approval design stops
   contradicting the wall-clock cap. `max_usd` already behaves
   correctly because spend does not accrue while idle. **Highest
   value — it fired on this run.**
2. **Bound contract review by budget as well as rounds** →
   `loop.py` (consult `budget_exceeded()` between review rounds, and
   emit an event when a round is skipped for budget) **plus**
   `.claude/agents/cdd-evaluator.md` Mode 1 (an explicit ceiling: read
   the plan and spot-check; do **not** re-implement tickets — that is
   the Generator's job and the plan is not approved yet). Effect: the
   59%-before-ticket-1 shape becomes bounded, in both places that
   caused it.
3. **Fix the `->` false positive** → `enforce_authority.py:98`, add a
   lookbehind so `-` before `>` is not a redirect. Effect: the scanner
   stops failing closed on legal Python. Pair it with one line in
   `cdd-evaluator.md`: a denial ends the session, a workaround is a
   protocol violation. (The heredoc blind spot stays out of scope per
   `loop-protocol.md §3` — do not grow the hook into a shell parser.)
4. **Flush pre-gate artifacts in their own commit at approval** →
   `loop.py`, one `plan(loop):` commit when the gate clears. Effect:
   `feat(loop):` commits carry exactly one ticket, governance §1's "one
   domain per commit" holds, and the Evaluator's per-ticket Boundary
   audit becomes a check it can actually pass.
5. **Make the toy a faithful deployment** → `toy_project.sh` +
   `toy_experiment.sh`: copy `CLAUDE.md` and `skills/` alongside
   `.claude/`. Then decide the follow-on question the toy will surface
   immediately: the 7.0 mode skills' interactive Ask/Halt phase versus a
   headless loop Planner — either the loop Planner is told to skip the
   Ask phase (the `[/loop]` Goal Setter already did it), or the mode
   skills grow a "in loop mode" clause. Consider `machinery()` checking
   for `skills/` only after that decision; today's Planner fallback is
   deliberate and legal, so aborting on absence would be wrong.
6. **State budget authority once** → `Goal.md` template in the toy
   scaffolders and `skills/mode-loop/SKILL.md`: budgets live in
   `goal.json` and may be raised mid-loop; the prose is a summary, not
   the contract. Cheaper than keeping two files in sync.

Items 1–3 are already captured in `feedback-inbox.md` 2026-07-31;
items 4–6 are new from this reading.
